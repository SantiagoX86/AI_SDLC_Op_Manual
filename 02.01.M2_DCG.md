## 1. Document and Artifact Architecture

The Operating Manual will establish a flexible, purpose-driven artifact architecture using four functional categories:

1. **Governance, Planning, and Definition:** Project rules, plans, requirements, and design specifications.
2. **Technical Implementation:** Source code, configurations, scripts, and other implementation artifacts.
3. **Verification and Decision Records:** Reviews, testing evidence, approvals, findings, and decisions.
4. **Reference and Supporting Materials:** Templates, guides, examples, and supporting resources.

Artifact function will be distinguished from authority, organizational role, lifecycle status, and change-control requirements. More authoritative artifacts may require stricter controls, while working materials may permit greater modification flexibility. Detailed identification and change-control mechanisms are deferred to subsequent M2 stages.

Artifact applicability will use four levels: **Mandatory, Conditionally Required, Recommended, and Optional**. Requirements will account for project risk, complexity, lifecycle activities, team structure, and applicable external obligations. Individual developers and small teams may have different documentation expectations, but self-review will not be represented as independent review where independence is required.

Documentation may be consolidated, referenced, or maintained separately according to project needs, provided that required information, responsibilities, traceability, and evidence remain adequately preserved. Artifact expectations will support all seven approved SDLC phases through a flexible combination of lifecycle-based and activity-driven applicability.

Documentation depth, formality, and implementation methods may be proportionately tailored without compromising M1's non-tailorable governance principles or applicable binding requirements. AI-specific documentation expectations apply where AI materially affects project activities, artifacts, decisions, systems, or outcomes.

## 2. Document Structure and Identification

The Operating Manual will establish a common structural and formatting baseline with proportionate variation according to artifact purpose, authority, and function. Formal SDLC documents should use consistent identification, organization, and applicable document-control information, while records, technical artifacts, and supporting materials may follow purpose-specific or native formats. The existing document-formatting template will serve as a starting reference, with formatting examples treated as guidance rather than mandatory content. Similar content structures should follow consistent conventions, and redundant metadata should be avoided.

Document identification will follow principles of uniqueness, consistency, meaningful organization, stability, scalability, and proportional complexity. The Manual may provide recommended default identifiers and naming conventions without prescribing a universal format. Document titles and filenames should support recognition and traceability, with structured milestone- or lifecycle-based naming and authority indicators recommended where appropriate. Formal authority should remain identifiable through metadata or equivalent controlled information; filename indicators do not independently establish authority. Identifiers should remain stable across revisions, and unnecessary renaming or embedded version information should be discouraged.

Metadata expectations will be proportionate to artifact type. Formal control documents may require structured version, ownership, authority, status, and approval information, while technical artifacts may rely on native documentation conventions and associated repository records. Required information need not be duplicated where reliably maintained through another appropriate mechanism.

Document relationships and cross-references should distinguish governing, subordinate, supporting, and informative materials using stable identifiers, descriptive titles, and navigable references where practical. References do not independently transfer authority. Milestone-specific guidance may consolidate applicable governing controls as a primary working reference for AI-assisted execution, without replacing or overriding upstream requirements. Relationships must preserve sufficient traceability to support review of guidance against governing controls and subsequent execution against that guidance.

Controlled artifacts must have an identifiable authoritative source, distinguishable from drafts, working copies, historical versions, and other non-authoritative materials. Storage conventions will remain tool-neutral and should preserve reference stability. AI-accessible documents must not be assumed current or authoritative solely because they are available in context.
