## 1. Project Identity and Claim Posture

The **Standards-Aligned AI-Assisted SDLC Operating Manual** is a practical, reusable, project-level resource for conducting software-development activities that use AI assistants under defined human oversight, documentation, review, accountability, and traceability practices. The Manual is intended to apply across AI-assisted development interaction models, including conversational coding assistance, prompt-driven or “vibe coding” workflows, and other AI-supported development approaches, provided that human authority, review, accountability, traceability, and controlled project change are maintained. It is intended primarily for individuals and small teams and addresses inconsistent or weakly controlled AI-assisted development by providing a proportionate combination of normative project expectations and supporting guidance. Although it does not claim to be a formal enterprise framework, it is designed to help users apply enterprise-realistic workflows, documents, roles, procedures, and decision practices wherever feasible, including through clearly identified simulated organizational contexts when a real enterprise structure does not exist. It does not claim to be an independent standard, certification or compliance program, complete enterprise AI-governance framework, legal or audit advice, or a guarantee of secure software. Its external alignment will be described as informed by, mapped to, and traceable to selected practices from authoritative standards, without implying complete coverage, formal compliance, validation, certification, endorsement, or replacement of the original sources.

---

## 2. Intended Users and Applicability

The primary users of the **Standards-Aligned AI-Assisted SDLC Operating Manual** are individual developers, independent technical project owners, and members of small software-development teams who directly plan, build, review, or hand-off AI-assisted software projects. The manual is designed to support both solo and collaborative use through responsibilities that may be performed by one person or distributed among several participants. It is particularly suited to users seeking to develop quality software through disciplined, enterprise-realistic workflows, documentation, roles, procedures, and decision practices, including clearly identified simulated organizational contexts where no real enterprise structure exists. Users are expected to possess foundational software-development knowledge, but they are not expected to have specialized expertise in enterprise governance, compliance, audit, artificial intelligence, or cybersecurity.

## 4. Scope, Exclusions, and System Boundary

### 4.1 Lifecycle phases covered

The operating manual will cover the full project-level software-development lifecycle:

1. `Project initiation and authorization`
2. `Project planning and lifecycle baselining`
3. `Requirements and analysis`
4. `Architecture and design`
5. `Implementation, including code review`
6. `Verification and validation, including testing and security review`
7. `Release/deployment, , operational-handoff, project closure, and lessons learned`

AI-specific requirements will apply only where AI materially affects an activity, artifact, decision, system, or outcome. Project-specific milestones may organize work within or across these stages but are not themselves SDLC lifecycle phases.

### 4.2 AI-assisted activities covered

AI-assisted activities are within scope when AI materially contributes to project work through generation of project artifacts; proposed modification or transformation of existing artifacts; analysis and evaluation; recommendation and decision support; research and information synthesis; project planning support; testing and validation assistance; or generation of scripts, commands, workflows, and other automation mechanisms. The manual follows an **AI-assisted, human-executed** operating model: AI may perform substantive supporting work and propose outputs or actions, but humans retain responsibility for reviewing and implementing project changes, executing tests and other project-affecting actions, approving authoritative artifacts, and making material project decisions. AI-generated test evidence or fabricated project results are not permitted, and incidental AI use that does not materially influence a project activity, artifact, decision, system, or outcome falls outside AI-specific scope.

### 4.3 Security and governance subjects included

The operating manual will address security and governance subjects necessary to support standards-aligned, project-level AI-assisted software development, including secure software development; AI-use governance; human review and accountability; development-focused information and data protection; project-level access and authorization; proportionate change and configuration governance; security verification and vulnerability handling during development; traceability, provenance, and project records; third-party and dependency risk; secure release and operational handoff; project-level risk and exception governance; and continued alignment to the approved external standards and guidance baseline.

### 4.4 Explicit exclusions

Scope will remain limited to concerns materially connected to the development project and will not expand into enterprise-wide data governance, identity governance, records management, vendor management, vulnerability-management operations, penetration-testing programs, threat-intelligence functions, SOC operations, incident-response programs, post-handoff production operations, ongoing maintenance, system retirement, or other organizational governance functions except where needed to define development requirements, handoff expectations, or project boundaries.

### 4.5 Project-level vs. organizational AI-governance boundary

The operating manual governs AI-assisted activities and related governance within the scope of an individual software-development project and does not establish or administer broader organizational or enterprise AI-governance functions. A project will be subject to applicable organizational AI-governance requirements without becoming responsible for establishing or administering those requirements outside the project scope. Applicable organizational requirements, approvals, services, and constraints may therefore operate as external dependencies or boundary conditions that the project must identify and respect. The manual may establish project-level governance and additional discipline compatible with applicable organizational requirements. Where no real organizational structure exists, proportionate simulated organizational contexts may be used without expanding the manual into an enterprise-governance framework.

### 4.6 Enforceable scope-control principles

The approved project scope, exclusions, and system boundaries will govern all later milestones, deliverables, standards analysis, supporting materials, and implementation work unless deliberately revised. Later work may clarify, decompose, or add implementation detail within an approved scope area but may not introduce materially new responsibilities, governance domains, lifecycle obligations, or system boundaries implicitly. External standards and guidance may inform how approved in-scope subjects are addressed but do not independently expand project scope. Out-of-scope or externally governed functions may be referenced as requirements, dependencies, interfaces, constraints, handoffs, or boundary conditions without transferring ownership of those broader functions into the manual. Any material change to the approved scope, exclusions, or system boundaries must be treated as an explicit architectural decision, with the existing approved scope remaining authoritative until such a change is deliberately adopted.

## 5. Deliverable Relationship and Authority Model

The project will use a hub-and-support deliverable model centered on the Operating Manual, which serves as the primary operational authority among project deliverables. 

1. The **Operating Manual** establishes the project’s normative operational requirements, expectations, practices, and procedural guidance. 
2. The **Standards Crosswalk** supports traceability between Manual provisions and the approved external standards and guidance baseline.
3. The **Limited Enterprise Target-State Profile** provides informative enterprise-realistic context relevant to use of the Manual.
4. The **Technical Demonstration Repository** provides illustrative examples and implementations of selected Manual concepts.
5. The **Decision records and other project records** preserve supporting rationale, provenance, evidence, and project history.

Supporting deliverables may reference the Operating Manual and one another where useful for traceability, context, demonstration, or provenance, but such references do not transfer normative authority or independently create operational requirements. Supporting artifacts will remain bounded to their approved purposes and may not become competing frameworks, hidden requirement sets, or implicit modifications of the Manual. Where a supporting project artifact is inconsistent with the Operating Manual, the Manual controls within the internal deliverable structure unless deliberately revised through the applicable governance process. Unnecessary duplication of authoritative guidance should be avoided in favor of appropriate references to the Manual. Approved external standards and guidance inform the Operating Manual, while the Standards Crosswalk documents that relationship; project artifacts do not replace, redefine, or supersede the authoritative external sources.

## 6. Minimum Viable Version 1.0 Boundary

Version 1.0 will establish a complete, coherent, and practically usable baseline for conducting an in-scope AI-assisted software-development project across the approved project lifecycle. Lifecycle coverage will be proportionate to the project’s actual responsibilities and authority: activities performed directly by developers or project participants will receive substantive operational guidance, while externally owned organizational functions may be addressed through requirements, roles, dependencies, interfaces, approvals, handoffs, or proportionate simulation where appropriate. V1.0 must be operationally implementable and may provide recommended templates, naming conventions, workflow formats, and other default methods without making those exact implementations mandatory unless required to satisfy an approved Manual requirement, applicable organizational requirement, or independently applicable binding requirement.

V1.0 will include the Operating Manual, Standards Crosswalk, Limited Enterprise Target-State Profile, necessary decision and project records, and supporting materials required to make mandatory Manual requirements practically usable. The Technical Demonstration Repository may be deferred to V1.1. The specific inventory of required supporting materials will be derived during later project work from the Manual’s actual requirements rather than exhaustively defined during M1. The Standards Crosswalk will support the project’s standards-informed claim posture and provide sufficient traceability to show that the Manual remains consistent with applicable guidance from the approved baseline, without serving as a compliance, certification, verification, or validation mechanism. V1.0 will be considered complete when the Manual and required supporting materials form a usable baseline with no known material gaps that would prevent an intended user from conducting a complete in-scope project; later versions may add depth, specialization, automation, demonstrations, additional examples, and other enhancements.

## 7. Operating Principles, Tailoring, and Governance Architecture

The Manual will operate under a non-tailorable governance floor consisting of human accountability, controlled AI assistance, meaningful human review, traceability and evidence, secure and disciplined development, clear responsibility and authority, proportional implementation, minimal duplication, and practical tool neutrality. AI may substantively assist project work but does not hold project authority; humans remain accountable for material decisions, approvals, authoritative artifacts, implementation, testing, and project outcomes. Where applicable project, organizational, or external requirements call for independent human review or separation of duties, self-review does not satisfy that requirement and must not be represented as equivalent.

Implementation rigor may be proportionately tailored according to project risk, complexity, scale, lifecycle context, team structure, and applicable external constraints. Tailoring may vary formality, documentation depth, review intensity, evidence, role separation, and implementation methods, but may not remove the Manual’s non-tailorable principles, override applicable organizational requirements, or substitute for deliberate authorization of a material architectural change.

Approved project decisions and authoritative artifacts remain controlling until deliberately changed. Applicable organizational requirements govern project execution and take precedence over conflicting project-level practices and non-binding external guidance. Conflicts between organizational requirements and the approved external-standards baseline must be escalated to appropriate project, management, or change-governance authority. Where such a conflict may involve a legally, regulatorily, contractually, or otherwise binding requirement, affected work must be paused pending authoritative review and resolution.

Detailed review procedures, tailoring criteria or matrices, role definitions, approval and escalation workflows, change-control mechanisms, and methods for documenting exceptions or deviations are deferred to later project work.

## 8. Provisional Milestone Architecture

The project will be organized into six milestones (M1–M6), following a functional-domain development approach. M1 establishes the foundational project architecture, while M2–M6 progressively develop, integrate, evaluate, and finalize the Operating Manual and its supporting deliverables. The milestone structure is intended to provide sufficient organization and control without unnecessary fragmentation or duplication of previously approved architectural work.

1. **M1 — Project Identity, Scope, and Governance Architecture:** Establish the project's identity, intended users, external standards baseline, scope and exclusions, system boundaries, deliverable authority model, minimum viable V1.0 expectations, operating principles, tailoring and governance architecture, and provisional milestone plan.

2. **M2 — Documentation and Configuration Governance:** Develop the Manual's documentation and configuration-control guidance, including required and optional SDLC documents, document structures, templates, naming conventions, control hierarchies, versioning, and controlled changes. Establish the initial Manual document structure and supporting standards-traceability mechanisms.

3. **M3 — AI-Assisted Development and Repository Operations:** Develop guidance for AI-platform organization, branching strategies, naming conventions, interaction and context controls, GitHub operations, repository hygiene, pull requests, and working-environment architecture and documentation.

4. **M4 — Human Review, Testing, and Validation:** Develop guidance for reviewing AI-generated code and SDLC documentation, testing methodologies, testing records and templates, evidence integrity, and proportionate human review practices for individual developers and small teams.

5. **M5 — Milestone Management, Closeout, and Handoff:** Develop guidance for milestone-specific context and guidance documents, milestone execution controls, closeout audits and documentation, release readiness, deployment, and operational handoff.

6. **M6 — Standards Alignment and V1.0 Finalization:** Finalize the Standards Crosswalk, Limited Enterprise Target-State Profile, and remaining required supporting materials; evaluate cross-artifact consistency and practical usability; resolve material gaps; verify alignment with the approved external guidance baseline; and complete final V1.0 review and approval.

The milestone sequence reflects the development of the Manual rather than the execution order of activities by future Manual users. Standards traceability and consistency will be maintained throughout development, with comprehensive evaluation performed during M6.

Detailed milestone objectives, in-scope decisions, required inputs, deliverables, exclusions, approval criteria, dependencies, risks, and completion evidence will be established when each milestone begins. All milestones will remain subject to the approved M1 architectural constraints and deliverable authority hierarchy.

The Technical Demonstration Repository may be deferred to V1.1 as previously approved.

## 9. M1 Final Review and Approval

Milestone 1 underwent an integrated architectural review and an external standards-alignment audit against the approved guidance baseline. The review evaluated internal consistency, scope and lifecycle boundaries, deliverable authority, Version 1.0 expectations, operating principles, tailoring, governance, milestone dependencies, architectural risks, and deferred decisions. Two minor clarifications were identified and addressed concerning the explicit inclusion of operational handoff in the lifecycle boundary and the distinction between external guidance and independently applicable binding requirements. No remaining material architectural conflicts or gaps were identified that would prevent progression to M2.

The external standards audit identified several nuanced considerations requiring continued attention during later milestones:

1. **Human review and accountability (M4):** Solo developer self-review must remain distinguishable from independent human review or separation of duties required by applicable external guidance. The Manual must not misrepresent self-review as satisfying independent-review requirements, and must specify where such exceptions are made for use by solo developers.
2. **Lifecycle boundaries and operational handoff (M5):** Post-release security responsibilities, including ongoing vulnerability response, must be appropriately acknowledged through requirements, dependencies, or handoff expectations without expanding the Manual into post-handoff organizational operations.
3. **Standards applicability and traceability (M2–M6):** External guidance must be evaluated according to applicability, approved scope, and authority. The Standards Crosswalk must distinguish adopted requirements, conditional applicability, and exclusions without implying comprehensive compliance or coverage. Source versions must be recorded to preserve traceability.
4. **Specialized expertise and proportional implementation (M2–M6):** Requirements involving specialized security or governance expertise must be addressed through appropriate responsibilities, external support, or documented limitations without imposing unnecessary enterprise-level complexity on individual developers and small teams.

These considerations are implementation and alignment concerns appropriately assigned to later milestones rather than unresolved M1 architectural decisions.

**Final Determination:** The M1 architecture is internally coherent, appropriately bounded, and sufficient to support subsequent project development. The milestone is approved for closure, and progression to **M2 — Documentation and Configuration Governance** is authorized, subject to recording the final approval and associated document-control updates.
